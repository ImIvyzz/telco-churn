Telco Customer Churn: who's leaving, why, and what it costs¶

Ivan Ignacio Velazquez · Data Analyst Originally built as my capstone project at Toronto School of Management (2024). Revised in November 2026 (v2) with corrected modeling, proper evaluation metrics and a business-impact layer.

Business question: a telecom provider loses about one in four customers. Which customers are most likely to leave, what drives it, and how much revenue could a retention program protect?

Dataset: IBM Telco Customer Churn sample (7,043 customers, 21 attributes: demographics, services, contract, billing and churn flag).

Key results¶
Churn rate 26.5% of customers, taking 30.5% of monthly revenue ($139k of $456k) with them
Biggest driver Contract type: 42.7% churn on month-to-month vs. 2.8% on two-year contracts
Riskiest moment The first year: 47.4% churn in months 0–12 vs. 14.0% after month 24
Model Logistic regression: ROC-AUC 0.84, catches 80% of churners (recall) vs. 0% for the "everyone stays" baseline
Targeting The top risk band is 33% of customers but holds 71% of all churners
Impact (scenario) Retaining 20% of high-risk churners protects ~$20.8k/month (~$250k/year) of revenue

4. Which differences are statistically real?¶

A chi-square test of independence for each categorical variable against churn. A p-value above 0.05 means we cannot claim the variable is related to churn.

5. Predictive model¶

Design choices:

  • Stratified 70/30 split, so train and test keep the 26.5% churn share.
  • Everything inside a scikit-learn Pipeline: the scaler and the encoder are fitted on training data only, so no information leaks from the test set.
  • A baseline that always predicts "stays". Any model must beat it, and accuracy alone is misleading here: the baseline already scores ~73%.
  • Recall and ROC-AUC as main metrics: the business cost of missing a customer who leaves is higher than the cost of a retention offer to one who would have stayed.
In [10]:
features = df.drop(columns=["Churn", "tenure_group"])
target = df["Churn"]
num_cols = ["tenure", "MonthlyCharges", "TotalCharges"]
cat_cols = [c for c in features.columns if c not in num_cols]

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    features, target, test_size=0.3, stratify=target, random_state=RANDOM_STATE)

preprocess = ColumnTransformer([
    ("num", StandardScaler(), num_cols),
    ("cat", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", drop="if_binary"), cat_cols),
])

models = {
    "Baseline (always 'stays')": DummyClassifier(strategy="most_frequent"),
    "Logistic regression": LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000, class_weight="balanced"),
    "Random forest": RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=400, min_samples_leaf=5,
                                            class_weight="balanced",
                                            random_state=RANDOM_STATE, n_jobs=-1),
}
pipes = {name: Pipeline([("prep", clone(preprocess)), ("model", m)]) for name, m in models.items()}
print(f"Train: {len(X_train):,} · Test: {len(X_test):,} · Churn share in test: {y_test.mean():.1%}")

Train: 4,930 · Test: 2,113 · Churn share in test: 26.5%
In [11]:
cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=RANDOM_STATE)
rows = []
for name, pipe in pipes.items():
    cv_auc = cross_val_score(pipe, X_train, y_train, cv=cv, scoring="roc_auc")
    pipe.fit(X_train, y_train)
    proba = pipe.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]
    pred = (proba >= 0.5).astype(int)
    rows.append({
        "model": name,
        "CV ROC-AUC (train, 5-fold)": cv_auc.mean(),
        "accuracy": accuracy_score(y_test, pred),
        "precision": precision_score(y_test, pred, zero_division=0),
        "recall": recall_score(y_test, pred),
        "F1": f1_score(y_test, pred),
        "ROC-AUC": roc_auc_score(y_test, proba),
    })
results = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("model")
results.to_csv(OUT / "model_results.csv")

Choosing the decision threshold¶

A lower threshold flags more customers: it catches more real churners (recall) but sends more offers to customers who would have stayed (lower precision). The right point depends on the cost of the retention offer.

6. What drives churn?¶

Odds ratios from the logistic regression. A value of 2.0 means the feature doubles the odds of churning; 0.5 means it halves them. Numeric features are standardized, so their odds ratio is per one standard deviation. Caveat: tenure and TotalCharges are strongly correlated, so read their individual coefficients with care; the permutation importance below is more robust.

7. Business impact¶

To score every customer without grading the model on data it was trained on, I use out-of-fold predictions: each customer's risk comes from a model that never saw that customer.

8. Conclusions and recommendations¶

What drives churn

  1. Contract flexibility. Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7% and make up 88.6% of all churners; two-year contracts churn at 2.8%.
  2. The first year. Churn is 47.4% in the first 12 months and falls steadily with tenure (9.5% after four years).
  3. Fiber optic without support. Fiber customers churn at 41.9%, and customers without tech support or online security churn at roughly 42%, almost three times the rate of those who have them.
  4. Payment by electronic check (45.3% churn vs. 15–19% for the other methods).
  5. Gender and phone service do not matter (chi-square p = 0.49 and 0.34).

Model. Logistic regression and random forest perform the same (cross-validated ROC-AUC 0.844 vs. 0.845), so I kept the logistic regression: it is simpler and every prediction can be explained. At a 0.5 threshold it catches 80% of churners with 51% precision; lowering the threshold to 0.4 catches 86%.

Recommendations

  1. Contract migration offer: a discount or perk for month-to-month customers in the high-risk band who switch to a one-year contract.
  2. First-90-days onboarding for new customers: check-in calls, setup help, early satisfaction survey.
  3. Bundle tech support and online security with fiber plans, the segment with the highest churn and highest bills.
  4. Incentivize automatic payment (card or bank transfer) for electronic-check customers.
  5. Operationalize the score: load customer_risk_scores.csv into the CRM, refresh it monthly and trigger automatic retention tasks for the high-risk band.

Limitations: a single snapshot (no time dimension), a public sample dataset, and observational data: these are associations, not proven causes. The retention scenario depends on the assumed save rate and does not subtract the cost of the offer.
